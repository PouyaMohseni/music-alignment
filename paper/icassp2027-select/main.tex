\title{SELECTION, NOT PERCEPTION: LEARNING TO CHOOSE DETECTOR CANDIDATES\\FOR ROBUST SHEET-IMAGE SCORE FOLLOWING}
\name{Pouya Mohseni \tbd{and co-authors}}
\address{\tbd{Schulich School of Music, McGill University, Montreal, Canada}}

\begin{abstract}
Real-time score following on sheet-music images needs no symbolic score, but
systems trained on synthesized audio lose accuracy on real recordings. We show
that for a state-of-the-art detection-based tracker this loss is a failure of
selection rather than perception: on real room-microphone piano recordings, a
correct candidate is among the detector's top 256 boxes at every scored onset,
and a causal oracle over them tracks 99.6\% of onsets within 0.5\,s, while the
detector's argmax tracks 80.0\%. We therefore freeze the detector and learn to
select. A parameter-free transition prior and a 23k-parameter set-based
candidate scorer, trained only on synthesized audio, raise accuracy on the real
recordings from 80.0\% to 94.9\%, above a recent end-to-end system that also
needs layout annotations, with significant gains on 80 held-out pieces under
noise. Teacher forcing shows that two thirds of the remaining error is error
propagation, which history-independent candidate features reduce.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Score following, the real-time alignment of a live performance to its score,
underlies automatic page turning~\cite{arzt2008}, computer
accompaniment~\cite{dannenberg1984,raphael2010} and practice tools. Classical
systems align audio to a symbolic score~\cite{cont2010,dixon2005match,nakamura2015}.
Most music, however, exists only as printed or scanned sheets, and converting
them to a symbolic form by optical music recognition remains
error-prone~\cite{calvo2020omr}. A line of work therefore follows the
performance directly on the sheet image, mapping the audio and a score page to
the current position~\cite{dorfer2016,dorfer2018rl,henkel2019rl,henkel2020cunet,henkel2021eusipco,henkel2021frontiers,yang2026coda}.

These systems are trained on audio synthesized from the score, and their main
open problem is real recordings. On the public set of real piano performances
aligned to sheet images~\cite{henkel2019rl}, the detection-based tracker
CYOLO-SB~\cite{henkel2021frontiers} drops from 89.3\% of onsets tracked within
0.5\,s on synthetic audio to 79.9\%, and the U-Net tracker CUNet from 85.5\% to
22.4\%~\cite{yang2026coda}. The remedies proposed so far change the training:
room impulse-response augmentation~\cite{henkel2021eusipco}, additional
proprietary recordings~\cite{henkel2021frontiers}, or a new cascaded
architecture that also consumes system and bar layout annotations at test
time~\cite{yang2026coda}.

We start from a measurement instead. The CYOLO-SB detector proposes 8,112
candidate note boxes per step and the tracker reports the most confident one.
On the real room-microphone recordings, a box within the 0.5\,s tolerance is
among the 256 most confident candidates at every one of the 4,149 scored
onsets, and an oracle that knows the answer but must commit one step at a time
and may never move backwards tracks 99.6\% of them. The argmax tracks 80.0\%
(Fig.~\ref{fig:example}). The information needed to follow the performance
survives the room; what fails is the rule that reads a position out of it. For
this model, robustness to real audio is a selection problem.

Our contributions are:
(i)~a diagnosis of the synthetic-to-real gap of a sheet-image tracker by
candidate-set oracles, which locates the gap in selection;
(ii)~a lightweight decoder for the frozen detector, a parameter-free transition
prior and a 23k-parameter set-based scorer (1.6\% of the detector's parameters)
with neighbourhood features that describe whether a box looks like a real
notehead, which raises accuracy on the real recordings from 80.0\% to 94.9\%
without layout annotations or retraining the detector;
(iii)~an analysis of what remains: with teacher forcing, two thirds of the
remaining error is error propagation, and the features that reduce it are those
that do not depend on the tracker's own history.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Score following has long been cast as sequential inference over score
positions, with hidden (semi-)Markov models~\cite{cont2010,nakamura2015} or
online time warping~\cite{dixon2005match}. Sheet-image systems replace the
symbolic score with a page image. Early work localized the position in unrolled
staff strips~\cite{dorfer2016}; reinforcement-learning agents moved a
cursor~\cite{dorfer2018rl,henkel2019rl}; CUNet segmented the position on full
pages~\cite{henkel2020cunet}; CYOLO recast tracking as audio-conditioned object
detection, with FiLM~\cite{perez2018film} in a YOLO-style
detector~\cite{redmon2016yolo,henkel2021eusipco}, and CYOLO-SB added system and
bar predictions~\cite{henkel2021frontiers}. CODA~\cite{yang2026coda} predicts
system, bar and note in a cascade with learned transition penalties and beam
search, trained end to end with layout supervision. We instead leave the
detector untouched and re-rank its output, which is closer in spirit to learned
re-scoring of detections~\cite{hosang2017nms,hu2018relation} than to a new
tracker. Because our scorer conditions on its own past choices, it faces the
exposure bias of sequence prediction~\cite{bengio2015scheduled,ross2011dagger},
which Sec.~\ref{ssec:left} measures directly.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Candidates from a frozen detector}

A performance is processed in steps $t=1,\dots,T$. At each step the detector
receives the audio up to $t$ and the current page and outputs note boxes
$b_i=(x_i,y_i,w_i,h_i)$ with objectness $o_i$ on three anchors of a
$52\times52$ grid. We keep the $K=256$ most confident, $\mathcal{C}_t$. Staves
are concatenated along one horizontal axis, the unrolled coordinate $u_i$, so
that positions on either side of a line break are close. The baseline outputs
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\imath}_t=\arg\max_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t} o_i .
  \label{eq:argmax}
\end{equation}
Everything below reads only $\mathcal{C}_t$ and the decoder's own previous
choice $\hat u_{t-1}=u_{\hat\imath_{t-1}}$.

\subsection{Transition prior}

Between steps $\Delta_t$ frames apart, the performer moves forward by a roughly
predictable amount. We score the displacement $d_i=u_i-\hat u_{t-1}$ with a
floored Gaussian whose mean grows with elapsed time,
\begin{equation}
  \log\pi_i=\max\Big(-\frac{(d_i-\mu\rho_t)^2}{2\sigma^2},\,J\Big),\quad
  \rho_t=\mathrm{clip}\Big(\frac{\Delta_t}{\Delta_0},0.2,8\Big),
  \label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
with $\mu=6$\,px per reference step of $\Delta_0=5$ frames, $\sigma=18$\,px,
and a floor $J=-6$ that keeps jumps possible. The prior has no trained
parameters; its constants were set on a validation proxy. On its own, the hand
score $h_i=\log o_i+\log\pi_i$ already improves the tracker
(Table~\ref{tab:main}).

\subsection{Candidate scorer}

Each candidate is described by $\phi_i\in\mathbb{R}^{33}$ in five groups:
confidence (log objectness, its gap to the frame's best, rank); box geometry;
motion with respect to the history (normalized displacement, backward and
staff-change flags, residual to a constant-velocity extrapolation); relation to
the detector's own predicted system and bar boxes; and a \emph{neighbourhood}
group that asks whether the box looks like a real notehead: the number of other
candidates within 25 and 100\,px, the gaps to the nearest candidate on either
side, the rank of the box in reading order, its width and height relative to
the frame's median box, its aspect ratio, and its relative position inside the
predicted bar. Of these, only the confidence, geometry and neighbourhood groups
are independent of the tracker's history. We also read the 128-dimensional
feature $f_i$ from which the detector's head scores the box. A
permutation-invariant network~\cite{zaheer2017deepsets} embeds the set and
pools it into a context shared by all candidates:
\begin{align}
  e_i &= g\big([\phi_i;\,\mathrm{ReLU}(Wf_i)]\big),\qquad
  c=\Big[\tfrac{1}{K}{\textstyle\sum_j} e_j;\ \max_j e_j\Big],
  \label{eq:embed}\\
  s_i &= r\big([e_i;\,c]\big),
  \label{eq:score}
\end{align}
where $g$ and $r$ are two-layer perceptrons (64 and 32 units; 64 and 1),
$W\in\mathbb{R}^{64\times128}$ and the maximum is taken per dimension. The
context lets each candidate be judged against the others in its frame. The
scorer has 22,881 parameters, 1.6\% of the 1.46M-parameter detector.

\subsection{Training}

We run the frozen detector over the MSMD training pieces~\cite{dorfer2018msmd},
synthesized and convolved with measured room impulse
responses~\cite{traer2016}, and store $\mathcal{C}_t$ and $f_i$ at every onset.
Each candidate carries its error $\varepsilon_i$, the distance in frames between
the score time of its position and the true time. The target is soft,
$q_i\propto\exp(-\varepsilon_i/\tau)$ with $\tau=3$ frames, and the loss is the
listwise cross-entropy
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}=-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t} q_i\,\log\frac{\exp s_i}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{C}_t}\exp s_j}.
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
The history features are built from the best candidates of the previous steps
(teacher forcing). On 30\% of the samples the previous position is perturbed
with Laplace noise of scale 30\,px, so that the scorer also sees imperfect
histories. We train with AdamW (learning rate $3\cdot10^{-3}$, cosine
schedule, 40 epochs, batch 256) and keep the epoch with the best greedy
rollout on the validation pieces.

\subsection{Decoding}

At test time the learned and hand scores are blended,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\imath}_t=\arg\max_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t}\ \beta\,s_i+(1-\beta)\,h_i ,
  \label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}
with $\beta=0.7$ chosen on a validation proxy, and $\hat u_t$ becomes the
history of step $t+1$. Decoding is causal and greedy: one forward pass of the
scorer over 256 candidates per step.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}

\textbf{Data.} The detector is the released CYOLO-SB
checkpoint~\cite{henkel2021frontiers}, trained on synthesized MSMD audio with
impulse-response augmentation. We train the scorer on its candidates over the
353 MSMD training pieces and validate on the 19 validation pieces. The test set
is the real-performance subset of~\cite{henkel2019rl}: 16 pieces, 25 pages and
4,149 onsets played on a Yamaha AvantGrand N2 hybrid piano and captured with a
room microphone (Setting~II in~\cite{yang2026coda}). For a larger sample we
also use 80 pieces withheld from the scorer's training. The detector saw their
synthesized audio during its own training, so we render them with a real room
impulse response and additive noise at 12, 6, 3 and 0.5\,dB SNR (40,594 onsets
per level).

\textbf{Metric.} As in~\cite{henkel2021frontiers,yang2026coda}, we report the
ratio of onsets whose predicted position maps to a time within 0.05 to 5\,s of
the true time; 0.5\,s is the primary figure. Confidence intervals come from a
bootstrap over pieces~\cite{efron1993}, the independent unit (16 on the test
set).

\textbf{Protocol.} The standard protocol runs a tracker only at annotated
onsets. Because our decoder carries state, we also run it on every frame at
20\,fps and score the onset frames, which removes any access to annotated onset
timing (last row of Table~\ref{tab:main}).

\textbf{Model selection.} Retraining with another seed moves the test score by
about a point, so we report seed means, and we choose the single reported
checkpoint without the test set: the configuration with the best held-out mean
over seeds, then its best seed on held-out. The test set was read once.

\subsection{Main result}

Table~\ref{tab:main} compares the decoder with published systems on the same
real recordings. Our run of the released detector reproduces the published
CYOLO-SB figures to within 0.001 at every threshold, so the comparison is made
on the detector the published numbers describe. The transition prior alone,
without any training, adds 6.5 points at 0.5\,s, and the scorer adds another
8.4. The decoded tracker is more accurate than every published system at
every threshold, including CYOLO-SB+A, which uses extra training data, and
CODA, which retrains the whole model and reads layout annotations at test
time. Against the detector's argmax, the gain at 0.5\,s is 13.8 points under
the piece bootstrap, with a 95\% confidence interval of $[5.6, 23.0]$. Tracked
on every frame, without annotated onset timing, the decoder scores \tbd{X} at
0.5\,s \tbd{(to be filled from the every-frame run)}.

\subsection{Ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:abl} adds the decoder's parts one at a time. Each step helps on
the real recordings. The prior's gain is significant on its own (95\% CI
$[2.7, 10.0]$), and so is the scorer's gain over the prior ($[1.4, 7.6]$). The
per-candidate features raise the real-recording mean from 91.4 to between 92.7
and 93.2; with 16 pieces this last step is not separable from zero there, but on
the 80 held-out pieces every one of six seeds beats the featureless scorer with
an interval excluding zero (mean gain 3.2 points). The width of the feature
projection does not matter: the two widths are tied within seed noise on both
sets, and we report the wider one because it has the better held-out mean,
which is the rule we fixed before reading the test set.

\subsection{Robustness to noise}

Fig.~\ref{fig:analysis}b shows the held-out pieces under increasing noise. The
full scorer improves on the featureless one by 3.9, 5.7, 8.5 and 6.6 points at
12, 6, 3 and 0.5\,dB, each with a 95\% interval excluding zero. As a share of
the remaining error, the gain shrinks from 30\% at 12\,dB to 10\% at 0.5\,dB:
the candidate features help most where the detector's boxes are still
reliable.

\subsection{What is left}
\label{ssec:left}

To see where the remaining error lives, we run the same scorer with the true
previous position at every step (teacher forcing). It then reaches 98.0\%, and
a causal oracle over the same candidates reaches 99.6\%. The deployed tracker's
5.1-point error therefore splits into 3.1 points of error propagation, lost by
conditioning on its own mistakes, 1.6 points of ranking, and 0.4 points no
causal selector could recover (Fig.~\ref{fig:analysis}a). The scorer without
the neighbourhood features (Table~\ref{tab:abl}, fourth row; one checkpoint
each) splits 4.7 and 1.5: those features cut propagation by a third and leave
ranking unchanged. This is what one should
expect from what they measure. Displacement and velocity features are computed
from the previous position and mislead the scorer as soon as that position is
wrong, whereas whether a box looks like a real notehead does not depend on the
history at all. Recovering from a lost state directly is harder than it looks:
the chosen box's objectness does reveal that the tracker is lost (AUC 0.81),
but lost steps are about 5\% of all steps, so no threshold gives a precision
above 0.33, and gated re-localization lowered accuracy in every configuration
we tried. The remaining errors are mostly timing drift within two bars (190 of
212); jumps to a wrong staff fall from 90 with the featureless scorer to 19.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A state-of-the-art sheet-image tracker loses accuracy on real recordings mostly
because of how it selects a position, not because of what it perceives, and a
small decoder over its frozen candidates recovers most of the gap, reaching
94.9\% of onsets within 0.5\,s on real piano recordings. The limits are those
of the benchmark: as in prior work, the correct page is given and the audio
restarts at page turns; all performances are piano; and the real test set has
16 pieces, so differences of a point or two between decoder variants are not
resolvable on it, which is why we report seed means and a larger held-out set.
The analysis points to the next step: the remaining error is propagation, and
the evidence that reduces it is evidence that does not depend on the tracker's
history. Stronger evidence of that kind, such as agreement between the pitches
a candidate implies and those heard in the audio, is the natural extension.
Code and trained scorers will be released.
