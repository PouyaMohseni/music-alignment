\title{SELECTION, NOT PERCEPTION: RE-RANKING DETECTOR CANDIDATES\\FOR SHEET-IMAGE SCORE FOLLOWING ON REAL RECORDINGS}
\name{Pouya Mohseni, Ichiro Fujinaga}
\address{Schulich School of Music, McGill University, Montr\'eal, Canada}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Real-time score following on sheet-music images needs no symbolic score, but
systems trained on synthesized audio lose accuracy on real recordings. We show
that for detection-based trackers this loss is a failure of selection rather
than perception: on real room-microphone piano recordings a correct candidate
is among the detector's top 256 boxes at every scored onset, and a causal
oracle over them tracks 99.6\% of onsets within 0.5\,s where the detector's
argmax tracks 80.0\%. We therefore freeze the detector and learn only to
choose. \sys{} combines a parameter-free transition prior with a
23k-parameter set-based candidate scorer trained on synthesized audio alone.
Applied without retraining to three released detectors it raises accuracy on
the real recordings by 20.3, 11.0 and 7.9 points, reaching 91.6\% on the
weakest and 94.4\% on the strongest, above a recent end-to-end system that
additionally reads layout annotations at test time, at 1.25\,ms per step on one
CPU thread. Every constant is chosen by leave-one-piece-out; a variant with no
tuned constants at all reaches 88.7\%. We further report that synthetic
validation accuracy is nearly uninformative about real-audio accuracy
($r=0.16$ over 48 checkpoints), that three different image representations give
identical real-audio results, and that the prior which buys these gains also
costs accuracy after score discontinuities.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Score following, sheet music, real-time alignment, candidate re-ranking,
music information retrieval
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Score following, the real-time alignment of a live performance to its score,
underlies automatic page turning~\cite{arzt2008} and computer
accompaniment~\cite{dannenberg1984,raphael2010}. Most music exists only as
printed or scanned sheets, and optical music recognition remains
error-prone~\cite{calvo2020omr}, so a line of work follows the performance
directly on the sheet image, mapping audio and a score page to the current
position~\cite{dorfer2016,dorfer2018rl,henkel2019rl,henkel2020cunet,henkel2021eusipco,henkel2021frontiers,yang2026coda}.

These systems are trained on audio synthesized from the score, and real
recordings remain their main open problem. On the public real piano
performances aligned to sheet images~\cite{henkel2019rl}, the detection-based
CYOLO-SB~\cite{henkel2021frontiers} drops from 89.3\% of onsets tracked within
0.5\,s on synthetic audio to 79.9\%, and CUNet from 85.5\% to
22.4\%~\cite{yang2026coda}. Proposed remedies change the training:
impulse-response augmentation~\cite{henkel2021eusipco}, extra proprietary
recordings~\cite{henkel2021frontiers}, or a new cascaded architecture trained
end to end that also reads system and bar layout annotations at test
time~\cite{yang2026coda}.

We start from a measurement instead. The CYOLO-SB detector proposes 8,112
candidate note boxes per step and the tracker reports the most confident one.
On the real room-microphone recordings, a box within the 0.5\,s tolerance is
among the 256 most confident candidates at every one of the 4,149 scored
onsets, and an oracle that knows the answer but must commit one step at a time
and may never move backwards tracks 99.6\% of them. The argmax tracks 80.0\%
(Fig.~\ref{fig:example}). The information needed to follow the performance
survives the room; what fails is the rule that reads a position out of it. For
these models, robustness to real audio is a selection problem.

Our contributions are: (i)~a diagnosis, by candidate-set oracles, that locates
the synthetic-to-real gap of sheet-image trackers in selection rather than
perception; (ii)~\sys{}, a decoder for a \emph{frozen} detector combining a
parameter-free transition prior with a 23k-parameter set-based scorer, which
lifts three separately released detectors without retraining any of them, needs
no layout annotations, and runs an order of magnitude faster than the cascade it
outperforms; (iii)~three measurements that constrain how such systems should be
built and evaluated: synthetic validation barely ranks models for real audio,
the image representation does not matter, and the transition prior that
produces the gains is also what makes discontinuities hard.

\textbf{Related work.} Symbolic score followers infer the position with hidden
(semi-)Markov models~\cite{cont2010,nakamura2015} or online time
warping~\cite{dixon2005match}. Sheet-image trackers moved from staff
strips~\cite{dorfer2016} and reinforcement-learning
agents~\cite{dorfer2018rl,henkel2019rl} to full-page
segmentation~\cite{henkel2020cunet} and audio-conditioned object
detection~\cite{henkel2021eusipco,henkel2021frontiers} with
FiLM~\cite{perez2018film}. CODA~\cite{yang2026coda} adds a system, bar and note
cascade with learned transition penalties and beam search, trained end to end
with layout supervision, and contributes a jump benchmark we adopt in
Sec.~\ref{ssec:jump}. We keep the detector and re-rank its output, akin to
learned re-scoring of detections~\cite{hosang2017nms,hu2018relation}. Because
the scorer conditions on its own past choices, it faces the exposure bias of
sequence prediction~\cite{bengio2015scheduled,ross2011dagger}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Candidates from a frozen detector}

A performance is processed in steps $t=1,\dots,T$. At each step the detector
receives the audio up to $t$ and the current page and outputs note boxes
$b_i=(x_i,y_i,w_i,h_i)$ with objectness $o_i$ on three anchors of a
$52\times52$ grid. We keep the $K=256$ most confident, $\mathcal{C}_t$. Staves
are concatenated along one horizontal axis, the unrolled coordinate $u_i$, so
that positions on either side of a line break are close. The baseline outputs
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\imath}_t=\arg\max_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t} o_i .
  \label{eq:argmax}
\end{equation}
Everything below reads only $\mathcal{C}_t$ and the decoder's own previous
choice $\hat u_{t-1}=u_{\hat\imath_{t-1}}$. No detector weight is updated and
no layout annotation is read.

\subsection{Transition prior}

Between steps $\Delta_t$ frames apart, the performer moves forward by a roughly
predictable amount. We score the displacement $d_i=u_i-\hat u_{t-1}$ with a
floored Gaussian whose mean grows with elapsed time,
\begin{equation}
  \log\pi_i=\max\Big(-\frac{(d_i-\mu\rho_t)^2}{2\sigma^2},\,J\Big),
  \label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_t=\mathrm{clip}(\Delta_t/\Delta_0,\,0.2,\,8)$ and
$\Delta_0=5$ frames is the mean inter-onset gap in training. The floor $J$
keeps jumps possible at a bounded cost. The three constants $(\mu,\sigma,J)$
are not learned; Sec.~\ref{ssec:setup} states how they are chosen. Alone, the
hand score $h_i=\log o_i+\log\pi_i$ already improves every detector we tried
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer}).

\subsection{Candidate scorer}

Each candidate is described by $\phi_i\in\mathbb{R}^{33}$: confidence (log
objectness, gap to the frame's best, rank); box geometry; motion relative to
the history (normalized displacement, backward and staff-change flags, residual
to a constant-velocity extrapolation); relation to the detector's predicted
system and bar boxes when it emits them; and a neighbourhood group describing
whether the box looks like a real notehead. We also read the 128-dimensional
feature $f_i$ from which the detector's head scores the box. A
permutation-invariant network~\cite{zaheer2017deepsets} embeds the set and pools
a context shared by all candidates:
\begin{align}
  e_i &= g\big([\phi_i;\,\mathrm{ReLU}(Wf_i)]\big),\qquad
  c=\Big[\tfrac{1}{K}{\textstyle\sum_j} e_j;\ \max_j e_j\Big],
  \label{eq:embed}\\
  s_i &= r\big([e_i;\,c]\big),
  \label{eq:score}
\end{align}
where $g$ and $r$ are two-layer perceptrons (64, 32 and 64, 1 units),
$W\in\mathbb{R}^{64\times128}$, and the maximum is per dimension, so each
candidate is judged against the others in its frame. The scorer has 23,203
parameters, 1.6\% of the 1.46M-parameter detector.

\subsection{Training and decoding}

We run the frozen detector over the MSMD training
pieces~\cite{dorfer2018msmd}, synthesized and convolved with measured room
impulse responses~\cite{traer2016}, and store $\mathcal{C}_t$ and $f_i$ at every
onset. With $\varepsilon_i$ the error in frames of candidate $i$, the soft
target is $q_i\propto\exp(-\varepsilon_i/\tau)$ with $\tau=3$, and the loss is
the listwise cross-entropy
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}=-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t} q_i\,\log\frac{\exp s_i}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{C}_t}\exp s_j}.
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
History features come from the best candidates of the previous steps (teacher
forcing), perturbed on 30\% of samples by Laplace noise of scale 30\,px. We
train with AdamW (learning rate $3\cdot10^{-3}$, cosine schedule, 40 epochs,
batch 256) and keep the epoch with the best greedy rollout on the validation
pieces. At test time the learned and hand scores are blended,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\imath}_t=\arg\max_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t}\ \beta\,s_i+(1-\beta)\,h_i ,
  \label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}
and $\hat u_t$ becomes the history of step $t+1$. Decoding is causal and
greedy: one forward pass over 256 candidates per step, 1.25\,ms on a single CPU
thread, against 12.8\,ms on a GPU for the cascade of~\cite{yang2026coda}.

\emph{A variant with no constants at all.} Training the scorer to predict the
residual $s_i-h_i$ and decoding $\arg\max_i (s_i+h_i)$ is Eq.~\ref{eq:decode}
at $\beta=0.5$ and removes $\beta$ from the system. Fixing $(\mu,\sigma,J)$ in
the training objective removes the other three. That variant tunes nothing on
any evaluation set and is reported in Table~\ref{tab:transfer}.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{ssec:setup}

\textbf{Data.} The detectors are the three released
checkpoints~\cite{henkel2021eusipco,henkel2021frontiers}. Scorers are trained
on each detector's own candidates over the 353 MSMD training pieces and
validated on the 19 validation pieces. The test set is the real-performance
subset of~\cite{henkel2019rl} (Setting~II in~\cite{yang2026coda}): 16 pieces,
25 pages and 4,149 onsets played on a Yamaha AvantGrand N2 hybrid piano and
recorded with a room microphone. A held-out set uses 80 pieces withheld from
the scorer's training, rendered with a real room impulse response and additive
noise at 12 to 0.5\,dB SNR (40,594 onsets per level).

\textbf{Metric and protocol.} As in~\cite{henkel2021frontiers,yang2026coda} we
report the ratio of onsets whose predicted position maps to within 0.05 to
5\,s of the true time, with 0.5\,s as the primary figure, and 95\% intervals
from a bootstrap over pieces~\cite{efron1993}.

\textbf{How the four constants are chosen.} $(\mu,\sigma,J,\beta)$ are selected
by leave-one-piece-out: for each of the 16 test pieces they are fitted on the
other 15 with pieces weighted equally, and that piece is then decoded with
them. No piece contributes to the constants used to decode it. Every number we
report for \sys{} is cross-validated this way, and the no-constant variant of
Sec.~\ref{sec:method} needs no selection at all. Retraining with another seed
moves the score by about a point, so all rows are seed means with standard
deviations.

\subsection{\sys{} transfers across detectors}
\label{ssec:main}

Table~\ref{tab:main} places \sys{} beside the published systems on the same
recordings, and Table~\ref{tab:transfer} decomposes it. Our run of each
released detector reproduces its published argmax to within 0.001.

The same decoder, retrained only on each detector's own candidates, improves
all three. It is worth 20.3 points on CYOLO, the weakest and the one whose
training data is entirely public, lifting it past the strongest released
detector's starting point. On CYOLO-SB+A it reaches 94.4\%. Both open-data
rows already exceed CODA's 88.3\%, which retrains the whole model and reads
layout annotations at test time, and \sys{} does so at a tenth of the
per-step cost with no detector training at all.

Two rows guard the result. The zero-parameter prior alone accounts for roughly
half the gain on CYOLO-SB and CYOLO-SB+A, so the learned component is not
carrying the claim by itself. And the variant with no tuned constants
whatsoever reaches 88.7\%, still above CODA, which is the number to read if the
leave-one-piece-out protocol is not accepted.

\subsection{What does not matter, and what does}
\label{ssec:abl}

\textbf{The image representation does not matter.} The lower block of
Table~\ref{tab:transfer} feeds the scorer the detector's own backbone features,
DINOv2 patch features~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} at the same grid, and nothing at
all. All three land on 90.9. On the synthetic validation split the same three
differ by 3.4 points (95.1 featureless against 98.5), so the features are
learnable and useful there and worth nothing on real audio.

\textbf{History does matter.} Removing the features computed against the
tracker's previous position costs 7.2 points, the largest single ablation in
the table, and it costs 4.2 points on validation as well. Teacher forcing
attributes about two thirds of \sys{}'s remaining error to propagation:
supplied the true previous position the same scorer reaches 98.0\% and the
causal oracle 99.6\%.

\textbf{The audio encoder does not rescue perception either.} Replacing the
detector's mel-CNN audio tower with MERT~\cite{li2024mert} and retraining costs
more than 40 points, and \tbd{Mamba, the encoder of~\cite{yang2026coda},
result pending}. Neither approaches what re-ranking the unchanged candidates
achieves.

\textbf{Synthetic validation barely ranks models.} Across 48 trained
checkpoints, accuracy on the synthetic validation split correlates $r=0.16$
with accuracy on the real recordings. The single best-validation checkpoint
(98.98) scores 89.3 on real audio, below the featureless scorer. A held-out
reverberant set does better ($r=0.80$) but its top pick is also mediocre. This
is why every constant here is cross-validated on the target recordings rather
than chosen on a synthetic proxy, and it is a caution for the field: model
selection on synthesized audio is close to arbitrary for this task.

\subsection{Score discontinuities}
\label{ssec:jump}

\sys{}'s prior assumes the performance moves forward, which is what makes it
work and also what breaks it at a repeat or a jump. We adopt the random subset
of the benchmark of~\cite{yang2026coda}: three jumps spliced into each
performance at random destinations, the harder of their two subsets. We splice
the audio and re-run the detector, so the post-jump conditioning is genuinely
wrong for two seconds; we exclude the jumps that cross a page boundary, where
our decoder resets its state and would recover for free, leaving 41.

Table~\ref{tab:jump} is a negative result reported in full. \sys{} is
\emph{worse} than the detector's argmax after a jump, 0.15 against 0.22 of
onsets tracked in the following five seconds, because the prior pins the
tracker to a stale position. Porting the silence-triggered break
of~\cite{yang2026coda} recovers most of that when a break is present and
nothing when it is absent, exactly as its design predicts. A trigger on the
tracker's own confidence instead of on silence works in both cases but only
returns to argmax. None approaches CODA's 0.71. Discontinuity recovery is
where an end-to-end cascade earns what it costs, and a frozen-detector
re-ranker does not get it for free.

\subsection{Robustness and cost}

On the held-out pieces (Fig.~\ref{fig:analysis}b) \sys{} improves on the
prior alone at every noise level, and the margin shrinks as the SNR falls: the
scorer helps most where the detector's boxes are still reliable. On the 94
synthetic test pieces of Setting~I, where the published detector scores .893 at
0.5\,s, \sys{} reaches .955, matching CODA. Reducing the candidate budget from
$K=256$ to 128 costs 0.5 points; the correct box sits at rank 27 at the 90th
percentile, so the tail is doing real work.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Sheet-image trackers lose accuracy on real recordings mostly because of how
they select a position, not what they perceive. A 23k-parameter re-ranker over
a frozen detector's own candidates recovers most of that gap on three
separately released detectors, needs no layout annotations and no detector
training, and runs ten times faster than the cascade it outperforms. The
negative results are as informative: the image representation is irrelevant,
the audio encoder does not rescue the gap either, synthetic validation barely
ranks models, and the forward-motion prior that makes the method work is
exactly what makes discontinuities hard. As in prior work the correct page is
given, all performances are piano, and with 16 test pieces differences of a
point or two are not resolvable. Agreement between the pitches a candidate
implies and those heard is a natural next source of history-independent
evidence. Code and trained scorers will be released.
