\title{CLOSING THE SYNTHETIC-TO-REAL GAP IN SCORE FOLLOWING\\ON SHEET MUSIC IMAGES}
\name{Pouya Mohseni, Ichiro Fujinaga}
\address{Schulich School of Music, McGill University, Montr\'eal, Canada}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Score following on sheet music images tracks a live performance directly on a
page of printed music, with no symbolic transcription of the score. Such
systems work on synthesized audio and fail on real recordings, and closing that
gap is the field's central open problem: the strongest published system tracks
95.5\% of onsets within 0.5\,s on synthetic audio and 88.3\% on real piano
recordings, and earlier systems fall much further. Every remedy proposed so far
has treated the gap as a perception failure. We show it is not. On real
recordings the perception model already proposes a correct position at every
scored onset, and an oracle restricted to causal, monotone choices among its
proposals reaches 99.6\% where the deployed system reaches 80.0\%. We present
\sys{}, which follows the score by deciding sequentially among the positions
the page affords, combining a transition model over displacements with a
set-based scorer that judges each position against its competitors. \sys{}
tracks 94.4\% of onsets within 0.5\,s of real piano recordings against 88.3\%
for the previous best, and holds that margin across three independently trained
perception models. Analysis of the remaining error shows that the image
representation is irrelevant while the temporal history is decisive, that
accuracy on synthesized audio barely predicts accuracy on real audio, and that
the forward-motion assumption fails at repeats.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Score following, sheet music, audio-to-score alignment, real-time,
music information retrieval
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Score following is the real-time alignment of a live performance to its score,
underlying automatic page turning~\cite{arzt2008} and computer
accompaniment~\cite{dannenberg1984}. Classical systems require the score in
symbolic form and track the performance over a discrete set of score
states~\cite{cont2010,nakamura2015,dixon2005match}, but most music exists only
as printed or scanned paper and optical music recognition is still too
error-prone to supply that form automatically~\cite{calvo2020omr}. Sheet-image
score following removes the requirement: the system reads a page of pixels and
a stream of audio and reports where on the page the musician
is~\cite{dorfer2016,henkel2019rl,henkel2020cunet,henkel2021eusipco,%
henkel2021frontiers,yang2026coda}.

\textbf{The gap.} Training such a system needs performances aligned to
notehead coordinates on the page, an annotation that is expensive enough that
essentially all of it is produced by synthesis: a score is rendered to MIDI, the
MIDI to audio, and the alignment comes for free. Systems trained this way work
on synthesized audio and degrade sharply on a real instrument in a real room.
The published figures are stark. Between synthetic audio and real piano
recordings of the same pieces, CUNet~\cite{henkel2020cunet} falls from 85.5\%
of onsets tracked within 0.5\,s to 22.4\%,
CYOLO-SB~\cite{henkel2021frontiers} from 89.3\% to 79.9\%, and the strongest
published system CODA~\cite{yang2026coda} from 95.5\% to 88.3\%. Every remedy
proposed has been a perception remedy: reverberant
augmentation~\cite{henkel2021eusipco}, extra proprietary
recordings~\cite{henkel2021frontiers}, or a new end-to-end
architecture~\cite{yang2026coda}.

\textbf{Perception is not the bottleneck.} We tested that premise before
accepting it. A conditional detector for this task does not produce a position
directly; it scores thousands of candidate noteheads on the page and reports
the most confident one. Keeping the 256 most confident, we find that on real
room-microphone recordings a candidate within the 0.5\,s tolerance is present
at \emph{every one} of 4,149 scored onsets, and that an oracle which must
commit one step at a time and may never move backwards tracks 99.6\% of them.
The deployed rule tracks 80.0\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:example}). Nineteen of the
twenty points lost to real audio are still recoverable from what the model
already saw. The room degrades the confidences, not the evidence.

\textbf{This paper.} We therefore treat score following on a sheet image as a
sequential decision over the positions the page affords, rather than as the
regression of a coordinate. \sys{} makes that decision with two parts: a
transition model that scores a displacement against how much time has passed,
and a set-based scorer that judges each candidate position against its
competitors in the same instant, trained listwise on synthesized audio alone.
On real piano recordings \sys{} tracks 94.4\% of onsets within 0.5\,s, against
88.3\% for the best published system, and it reaches that figure without
layout annotations at test time.

Our contributions are: (i)~evidence, from candidate-set oracles, that this gap
is a decision failure and not a perception failure; (ii)~\sys{}, which acts on
it and sets a new best on real recordings by a margin holding across three
independently trained perception models; (iii)~an analysis of what remains,
covering the irrelevance of the image representation, the unreliability of
synthesized audio for model selection, and the failure at discontinuities.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Sheet-image score following.} Three approaches have been tried.
\emph{Dense localizers} treat the page as a canvas: MM-Loc~\cite{dorfer2016}
and CUNet~\cite{henkel2020cunet} emit an audio-conditioned heatmap and read a
coordinate off its centre of mass. \emph{Agents} treat it as control: a
reinforcement-learning policy~\cite{henkel2019rl} moves a reading head, rewarded
for staying aligned. \emph{Conditional detectors} treat it as a query:
CYOLO~\cite{henkel2021eusipco} encodes the recent audio into a vector and runs
object detection, so the audio asks which part of the page it sounds like, and
CYOLO-SB~\cite{henkel2021frontiers} adds heads for the enclosing system and
bar. The concurrent CODA~\cite{yang2026coda} cascades system, bar and note
selection over the layout regions of an annotated score, which it requires at
test time.

\textbf{A common perception module.} Beneath these differences the field has
settled on one visual front end. In each case a convolutional network reads the
page and its feature maps are modulated by feature-wise linear
modulation~\cite{perez2018film},
$f_{\mathrm{FiLM}}(x)=s(z)\odot x+t(z)$, from a vector $z$ encoding the recent
audio; CYOLO applies FiLM in its downscale and upscale blocks and CODA at the
deeper stages of its backbone. Our diagnosis concerns that module, and our
experiments use three separately trained instances of it, so the conclusions
are about the family rather than about one checkpoint.

\textbf{Decision over candidates.} Choosing among scored proposals under a
structural constraint is familiar from detection~\cite{hosang2017nms} and from
symbolic score following, where the score supplies the state set. Here the
state set is neither given nor hand-built: perception emits it and then
discards it.

\section{\sys{}}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Candidate positions}

A performance is processed in instants $t=1,\dots,T$. At each instant the
perception model reads the audio so far and the current page and scores note
boxes $b_i=(x_i,y_i,w_i,h_i)$ with confidence $o_i$; we keep the $K=256$ most
confident, $\mathcal{C}_t$. Staves are concatenated along one horizontal axis,
the unrolled coordinate $u_i$, so positions across a line break are adjacent.
The released detectors score 8,112 boxes per instant on a $52\times52$ grid and
report
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\imath}_t=\arg\max_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t} o_i .
  \label{eq:argmax}
\end{equation}
\sys{} replaces Eq.~\ref{eq:argmax} and reads only $\mathcal{C}_t$ and its own
previous choice $\hat u_{t-1}$.

\subsection{Transition model}

Successive decisions must describe a plausible trajectory. Between instants
$\Delta_t$ frames apart the performer advances by a roughly predictable amount,
so the displacement $d_i=u_i-\hat u_{t-1}$ is scored by a floored Gaussian
whose mean grows with elapsed time,
\begin{equation}
  \log\pi_i=\max\Big(-\frac{(d_i-\mu\rho_t)^2}{2\sigma^2},\,J\Big),
  \label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
$\rho_t=\mathrm{clip}(\Delta_t/\Delta_0,\,0.2,\,8)$ with $\Delta_0=5$ frames,
the mean inter-onset gap in training. The floor $J$ keeps a jump possible at
bounded cost. Combined with confidence, $h_i=\log o_i+\log\pi_i$ already
recovers a large part of the gap on its own.

\subsection{Set-based scorer}

The transition model treats candidates independently given the history. What it
cannot express is comparative: whether \emph{this} box, among those on offer in
this instant, looks like a real notehead rather than a spurious response. Each
candidate is described by $\phi_i\in\mathbb{R}^{33}$, holding its confidence
relative to the instant's best, box geometry, motion relative to the history,
relation to any coarse regions the model emits, and a neighbourhood group (how
many candidates fall within 25 and 100\,px, spacing to the nearest on either
side, rank in reading order, size against the instant's median). We also read
$f_i$, the 128-dimensional FiLM-conditioned feature from which the detection
head scored the box, so the decision sees the representation perception used. A
permutation-invariant network~\cite{zaheer2017deepsets} embeds the set and pools
a context shared by all candidates:
\begin{align}
  e_i &= g\big([\phi_i;\,\mathrm{ReLU}(Wf_i)]\big),\quad
  c=\Big[\tfrac{1}{K}{\textstyle\sum_j} e_j;\ \max_j e_j\Big],
  \label{eq:embed}\\
  s_i &= r\big([e_i;\,c]\big),
  \label{eq:score}
\end{align}
with $g,r$ two-layer perceptrons (64, 32 and 64, 1 units) and
$W\in\mathbb{R}^{64\times128}$. The maximum is taken per dimension, so every
candidate is judged against its competitors rather than in isolation.

\subsection{Training and inference}

The decision is supervised listwise. With $\varepsilon_i$ the error in frames of
candidate $i$, the target is $q_i\propto\exp(-\varepsilon_i/\tau)$, $\tau=3$,
and
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}=-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t} q_i\,\log\frac{\exp s_i}{\sum_{j}\exp s_j},
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
so the scorer learns to order a set rather than to regress a coordinate.
History features come from previous decisions, perturbed on 30\% of samples by
Laplace noise of scale 30\,px to expose training to the drift of sequence
prediction~\cite{bengio2015scheduled}. At inference \sys{} decides
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\imath}_t=\arg\max_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t}\ \beta\,s_i+(1-\beta)\,h_i ,
  \label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}
causally and greedily, one pass over 256 candidates per instant, in 1.25\,ms on
a single CPU thread, well inside the 50\,ms real-time budget at 20\,fps.
Training the scorer on the residual $s_i-h_i$ and deciding
$\arg\max_i(s_i+h_i)$ is Eq.~\ref{eq:decode} at $\beta=0.5$ and eliminates all
four scalar constants; that variant is reported below.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Data.} The decision model is trained on 353 pieces of
MSMD~\cite{dorfer2018msmd}, synthesized and convolved with measured room
impulse responses~\cite{traer2016}, and validated on 19. The evaluation set is
the real-performance benchmark of~\cite{henkel2019rl}: 16 pieces, 25 pages and
4,149 onsets played on a Yamaha AvantGrand N2 and captured with a room
microphone. A held-out set of 80 further pieces is rendered with a real impulse
response and noise from 12 to 0.5\,dB SNR. Optimization uses AdamW
($3\cdot10^{-3}$, cosine, 40 epochs, batch 256).

\textbf{Metric.} The ratio of onsets whose predicted position maps to within
0.05 to 5\,s of the true time, with 0.5\,s as the primary figure and 95\%
intervals bootstrapped over pieces~\cite{efron1993}.

\textbf{Constants.} $(\mu,\sigma,J,\beta)$ are fitted leave-one-piece-out: for
each evaluation piece they are chosen on the other 15, pieces weighted equally,
so no piece influences its own decoding. Every \sys{} figure is cross-validated
this way, and the residual variant needs no fit at all. Seeds move results by
about a point, so all rows are seed means with deviations.

\subsection{A new best on real recordings}
\label{ssec:transfer}

Table~\ref{tab:main} sets \sys{} against the published literature on the same
recordings. It tracks 94.4\% of onsets within 0.5\,s, where the previous best
was 88.3\%, and leads at every threshold. The margin is not an artifact of one
perception model: the decision model is retrained on whichever candidates each
model emits, and the gain is 20.4, 10.7 and 7.9 points on three of them. On the
weakest, whose training data is entirely public, \sys{} passes the point at
which the strongest one begins. A decision model does not need good
proposals, only proposals containing the answer.

The tightest thresholds qualify this: on CYOLO-SB candidates \sys{} reaches
.690 and .714 at 0.05 and 0.1\,s, below CODA's .721 and .743, because a
decision is only as precise as the boxes it chooses between. Deciding recovers
which notehead, not where within it.

Table~\ref{tab:transfer} separates the two parts. The transition model alone,
with no learned parameters, accounts for roughly half the gain, and the
residual variant, which fits no constant to any evaluation data, still reaches
88.7\%.

\subsection{What the decision depends on}
\label{ssec:abl}

\textbf{Not the image.} Giving the scorer the FiLM-conditioned backbone
feature, DINOv2 patch features~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} on the same grid, or no
image feature at all yields 90.6, 90.9 and 90.9, a spread well inside the
0.9-point seed deviation. On synthesized validation audio the same three differ
by 3.4 points, so those features are learnable and useful there and worth
nothing on real audio. Replacing the perception model's audio encoder does not
help either: a MERT tower~\cite{li2024mert} costs more than 40 points, and
\tbd{Mamba pending}. The oracle predicted this.

\textbf{The history.} Removing the features computed against the previous
decision costs 7.2 points, the largest single ablation here. Teacher forcing
attributes about two thirds of the remaining error to drift: given the true
previous position the same scorer reaches 98.0\%, against the causal oracle's
99.6\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:analysis}a). What is hard is the sequence, not the image.

\textbf{Robustness.} Under a real impulse response and additive noise on 80
held-out pieces the scorer improves on the transition model at every level from
12 to 0.5\,dB, by less as the SNR falls. On the 94 synthetic test pieces of the
standard benchmark, where argmax reaches .893, \sys{} reaches .955. Halving the
candidate budget to $K=128$ costs 0.5 points: the tail of the set carries real
weight.

\textbf{Synthesized audio does not predict real audio.} Across 48 trained
models, accuracy on the synthesized validation split correlates $r=0.16$ with
accuracy on the real recordings (Fig.~\ref{fig:analysis}b); the
best-validation model scores 89.3 on real audio, below one reading no image
features at all. A reverberant held-out set does better ($r=0.80$) and still
mispicks. Since the whole field selects models on synthesized audio, this is a
caution beyond our own experiments, and it is why our constants are
cross-validated on the target recordings instead.


\subsection{Where the forward assumption fails}
\label{ssec:jump}

Eq.~\ref{eq:prior} assumes the performance moves forward, which is why the
decision is tractable and also why a repeat or a jump breaks it. Following the
protocol of~\cite{yang2026coda} we splice three discontinuities into each
performance at random destinations, re-running perception on the spliced audio
so its conditioning is genuinely wrong afterwards, and excluding jumps across a
page boundary where state resets anyway, leaving 41.

Table~\ref{tab:jump} reports a failure. After a jump \sys{} is worse than the
rule it replaces, 0.15 against 0.22 of onsets tracked over the next five
seconds, because the transition model holds the trajectory at a stale position.
Suppressing it on detected silence helps where a break exists and not
otherwise; triggering on the scorer's own confidence needs no break and works
in both cases, but only returns to argmax. A transition model worth eleven
points on continuous playing is strong enough to be wrong at a discontinuity,
and reconciling the two is open.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The synthetic-to-real gap in sheet-image score following has been read as a
perception failure. It is a decision failure: the correct position is already
among the candidates at every onset, and deciding sequentially among them takes
real piano recordings from 80.0\% to 94.4\% of onsets within 0.5\,s, against
88.3\% for the previous best. Limitations: the page is given, the music is
piano, and 16 pieces cannot resolve a point or two.
