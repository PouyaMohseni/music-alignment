\title{SELECTION, NOT PERCEPTION: LEARNING TO CHOOSE DETECTOR CANDIDATES\\FOR ROBUST SHEET-IMAGE SCORE FOLLOWING}
\name{Pouya Mohseni, Ichiro Fujinaga}
\address{Schulich School of Music, McGill University, Montr\'eal, Canada}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Real-time score following on sheet-music images needs no symbolic score, but
systems trained on synthesized audio lose accuracy on real recordings. We show
that for a state-of-the-art detection-based tracker this loss is a failure of
selection rather than perception: on real room-microphone piano recordings, a
correct candidate is among the detector's top 256 boxes at every scored onset,
and a causal oracle over them tracks 99.6\% of onsets within 0.5\,s, while the
detector's argmax tracks 80.0\%. We therefore freeze the detector and learn to
select. A parameter-free transition prior and a 23k-parameter set-based
candidate scorer, trained only on synthesized audio, raise accuracy on the real
recordings from 80.0\% to 94.9\%, above a recent end-to-end system that also
needs layout annotations, with significant gains on 80 held-out pieces under
noise. Teacher forcing shows that two thirds of the remaining error is error
propagation, which history-independent candidate features reduce.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Score following, the real-time alignment of a live performance to its score,
underlies automatic page turning~\cite{arzt2008} and computer
accompaniment~\cite{dannenberg1984,raphael2010}. Most music exists only as
printed or scanned sheets, and optical music recognition remains
error-prone~\cite{calvo2020omr}, so a line of work follows the performance
directly on the sheet image, mapping audio and a score page to the current
position~\cite{dorfer2016,dorfer2018rl,henkel2019rl,henkel2020cunet,henkel2021eusipco,henkel2021frontiers,yang2026coda}.

These systems are trained on audio synthesized from the score, and real
recordings remain their main open problem. On the public real piano
performances aligned to sheet images~\cite{henkel2019rl}, the detection-based
CYOLO-SB~\cite{henkel2021frontiers} drops from 89.3\% of onsets tracked within
0.5\,s on synthetic audio to 79.9\%, and CUNet from 85.5\% to
22.4\%~\cite{yang2026coda}. Proposed remedies change the training:
impulse-response augmentation~\cite{henkel2021eusipco}, extra proprietary
recordings~\cite{henkel2021frontiers}, or a new cascaded architecture that also
reads system and bar layout annotations at test time~\cite{yang2026coda}.

We start from a measurement instead. The CYOLO-SB detector proposes 8,112
candidate note boxes per step and the tracker reports the most confident one.
On the real room-microphone recordings, a box within the 0.5\,s tolerance is
among the 256 most confident candidates at every one of the 4,149 scored
onsets, and an oracle that knows the answer but must commit one step at a time
and may never move backwards tracks 99.6\% of them. The argmax tracks 80.0\%
(Fig.~\ref{fig:example}). The information needed to follow the performance
survives the room; what fails is the rule that reads a position out of it. For
this model, robustness to real audio is a selection problem.

Our contributions are: (i)~a diagnosis, by candidate-set oracles, that locates
the synthetic-to-real gap of a sheet-image tracker in selection; (ii)~a
lightweight decoder for the frozen detector, a parameter-free transition prior
and a 23k-parameter set-based scorer (1.6\% of the detector) with
neighbourhood features, which raises accuracy on the real recordings from
80.0\% to 94.9\% without layout annotations or retraining; (iii)~an analysis
showing that two thirds of the remaining error is error propagation, reduced by
the features that do not depend on the tracker's history.

\textbf{Related work.} Symbolic score followers infer the position with hidden
(semi-)Markov models~\cite{cont2010,nakamura2015} or online time
warping~\cite{dixon2005match}. Sheet-image trackers moved from staff
strips~\cite{dorfer2016} and reinforcement-learning
agents~\cite{dorfer2018rl,henkel2019rl} to full-page
segmentation~\cite{henkel2020cunet} and audio-conditioned object
detection~\cite{henkel2021eusipco,henkel2021frontiers} with
FiLM~\cite{perez2018film}. CODA~\cite{yang2026coda} adds a system, bar and note
cascade with learned transition penalties and beam search, trained end to end
with layout supervision. We keep the detector and re-rank its output, akin to
learned re-scoring of detections~\cite{hosang2017nms,hu2018relation}. Because
the scorer conditions on its own past choices, it faces the exposure bias of
sequence prediction~\cite{bengio2015scheduled,ross2011dagger}, which
Sec.~\ref{ssec:left} measures.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Candidates from a frozen detector}

A performance is processed in steps $t=1,\dots,T$. At each step the detector
receives the audio up to $t$ and the current page and outputs note boxes
$b_i=(x_i,y_i,w_i,h_i)$ with objectness $o_i$ on three anchors of a
$52\times52$ grid. We keep the $K=256$ most confident, $\mathcal{C}_t$. Staves
are concatenated along one horizontal axis, the unrolled coordinate $u_i$, so
that positions on either side of a line break are close. The baseline outputs
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\imath}_t=\arg\max_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t} o_i .
  \label{eq:argmax}
\end{equation}
Everything below reads only $\mathcal{C}_t$ and the decoder's own previous
choice $\hat u_{t-1}=u_{\hat\imath_{t-1}}$.

\subsection{Transition prior}

Between steps $\Delta_t$ frames apart, the performer moves forward by a roughly
predictable amount. We score the displacement $d_i=u_i-\hat u_{t-1}$ with a
floored Gaussian whose mean grows with elapsed time,
\begin{equation}
  \log\pi_i=\max\Big(-\frac{(d_i-\mu\rho_t)^2}{2\sigma^2},\,J\Big),
  \label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_t=\mathrm{clip}(\Delta_t/\Delta_0,\,0.2,\,8)$, $\mu=6$\,px per
reference step of $\Delta_0=5$ frames, $\sigma=18$\,px, and the floor $J=-6$
keeps jumps possible. The prior has no trained parameters
\tbd{(constants: provenance to be stated after the held-out re-selection)}.
Alone, the hand score $h_i=\log o_i+\log\pi_i$ already improves the tracker
(Table~\ref{tab:main}).

\subsection{Candidate scorer}

Each candidate is described by $\phi_i\in\mathbb{R}^{33}$: confidence (log
objectness, gap to the frame's best, rank); box geometry; motion relative to
the history (normalized displacement, backward and staff-change flags, residual
to a constant-velocity extrapolation); relation to the detector's predicted
system and bar boxes; and a \emph{neighbourhood} group asking whether the box
looks like a real notehead (candidates within 25 and 100\,px, gaps to the
nearest candidate on each side, rank in reading order, size and aspect relative
to the frame's median box, position inside the predicted bar). Only the
confidence, geometry and neighbourhood groups are independent of the history.
We also read the 128-dimensional feature $f_i$ from which the detector's head
scores the box. A permutation-invariant network~\cite{zaheer2017deepsets}
embeds the set and pools a context shared by all candidates:
\begin{align}
  e_i &= g\big([\phi_i;\,\mathrm{ReLU}(Wf_i)]\big),\qquad
  c=\Big[\tfrac{1}{K}{\textstyle\sum_j} e_j;\ \max_j e_j\Big],
  \label{eq:embed}\\
  s_i &= r\big([e_i;\,c]\big),
  \label{eq:score}
\end{align}
where $g$ and $r$ are two-layer perceptrons (64, 32 and 64, 1 units),
$W\in\mathbb{R}^{64\times128}$, and the maximum is per dimension, so each
candidate is judged against the others in its frame. The scorer has 22,881
parameters, 1.6\% of the 1.46M-parameter detector.

\subsection{Training and decoding}

We run the frozen detector over the MSMD training
pieces~\cite{dorfer2018msmd}, synthesized and convolved with measured room
impulse responses~\cite{traer2016}, and store $\mathcal{C}_t$ and $f_i$ at every
onset. With $\varepsilon_i$ the error in frames of candidate $i$, the soft
target is $q_i\propto\exp(-\varepsilon_i/\tau)$ with $\tau=3$, and the loss is
the listwise cross-entropy
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}=-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t} q_i\,\log\frac{\exp s_i}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{C}_t}\exp s_j}.
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
History features come from the best candidates of the previous steps (teacher
forcing), perturbed on 30\% of samples by Laplace noise of scale 30\,px. We
train with AdamW (learning rate $3\cdot10^{-3}$, cosine schedule, 40 epochs,
batch 256) and keep the epoch with the best greedy rollout on the validation
pieces. At test time the learned and hand scores are blended,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\imath}_t=\arg\max_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t}\ \beta\,s_i+(1-\beta)\,h_i ,
  \label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}
with $\beta=0.7$ \tbd{(provenance as for the prior)}, and $\hat u_t$ becomes the
history of step $t+1$. Decoding is causal and greedy: one forward pass of the
scorer over 256 candidates per step.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{ssec:setup}

\textbf{Data.} The detector is the released CYOLO-SB
checkpoint~\cite{henkel2021frontiers}. The scorer is trained on its candidates
over the 353 MSMD training pieces and validated on the 19 validation pieces.
The test set is the real-performance subset of~\cite{henkel2019rl} (Setting~II
in~\cite{yang2026coda}): 16 pieces, 25 pages and 4,149 onsets played on a
Yamaha AvantGrand N2 hybrid piano and recorded with a room microphone. A larger
held-out set uses 80 pieces withheld from the scorer's training; as the
detector saw their synthesized audio, we render them with a real room impulse
response and additive noise at 12, 6, 3 and 0.5\,dB SNR (40,594 onsets per
level).

\textbf{Metric and protocol.} As in~\cite{henkel2021frontiers,yang2026coda},
we report the ratio of onsets whose predicted position maps to within 0.05 to
5\,s of the true time, with 0.5\,s as the primary figure, and 95\% intervals
from a bootstrap over pieces~\cite{efron1993}. The standard protocol steps a
tracker only at annotated onsets; since our decoder carries state, we also step
it on every frame at 20\,fps and score the onset frames, which removes access
to annotated onset timing. Retraining with another seed moves the test score by
about a point, so we report seed means, and the single reported checkpoint is
chosen without the test set: the best held-out configuration mean, then its
best seed on held-out.

\subsection{Main result}

Table~\ref{tab:main} compares the decoder with published systems on the same
real recordings. Our run of the released detector reproduces the published
CYOLO-SB figures to within 0.001 at every threshold. The transition prior
alone, without training, adds 6.5 points at 0.5\,s, and the scorer another 8.4.
The decoded tracker is more accurate than every published system at every
threshold, including CYOLO-SB+A, which uses extra training data, and CODA,
which retrains the whole model and reads layout annotations at test time.
Against the detector's argmax, the gain at 0.5\,s is 13.8 points under the
piece bootstrap (95\% CI $[5.6, 23.0]$). Stepped on every frame, the decoder
scores \tbd{X} at 0.5\,s \tbd{(every-frame run pending; the argmax is
unchanged at .800 by construction)}.

\subsection{Ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:abl} adds the decoder's parts one at a time, and each helps on
the real recordings. The prior's gain is significant on its own (95\% CI
$[2.7, 10.0]$), and so is the scorer's gain over the prior ($[1.4, 7.6]$). The
per-candidate features raise the real-recording mean from 91.4 to between 92.7
and 93.2; with 16 pieces this step is not separable from zero there, but on the
80 held-out pieces all six seeds beat the featureless scorer with intervals
excluding zero (mean gain 3.2 points). The two projection widths are tied
within seed noise on both sets; we report the wider one because it has the
better held-out mean, the rule fixed before reading the test set.

\subsection{Robustness to noise}

On the held-out pieces (Fig.~\ref{fig:analysis}b) the full scorer improves on
the featureless one by 3.9, 5.7, 8.5 and 6.6 points at 12, 6, 3 and 0.5\,dB,
each with a 95\% interval excluding zero. As a share of the remaining error the
gain shrinks from 30\% to 10\%: the candidate features help most where the
detector's boxes are still reliable.

\subsection{What is left}
\label{ssec:left}

Given the true previous position at every step (teacher forcing), the same
scorer reaches 98.0\%, and the causal oracle 99.6\%. The deployed tracker's
5.1-point error thus splits into 3.1 points of error propagation, 1.6 of
ranking, and 0.4 no causal selector could recover (Fig.~\ref{fig:analysis}a).
The scorer without the neighbourhood features (Table~\ref{tab:abl}, fourth row;
one checkpoint each) splits 4.7 and 1.5: those features cut propagation by a
third and leave ranking unchanged. This matches what they measure. Displacement
and velocity features are computed from the previous position and mislead the
scorer once it is wrong, whereas whether a box looks like a real notehead does
not depend on the history. Recovering from a lost state directly is harder: the
chosen box's objectness does reveal that the tracker is lost (AUC 0.81), but
lost steps are about 5\% of all steps, so no threshold reaches a precision above
0.33, and gated re-localization lowered accuracy in every configuration we
tried. Most remaining errors are timing drift within two bars (190 of 212);
jumps to a wrong staff fall from 90 with the featureless scorer to 19.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A state-of-the-art sheet-image tracker loses accuracy on real recordings mostly
because of how it selects a position, not what it perceives, and a small
decoder over its frozen candidates recovers most of the gap, reaching 94.9\% of
onsets within 0.5\,s on real piano recordings. As in prior work, the correct
page is given and the audio restarts at page turns; all performances are piano;
and with 16 test pieces, differences of a point or two between variants are
not resolvable, hence the seed means and the larger held-out set. The remaining
error is propagation, and what reduces it is evidence independent of the
tracker's history; agreement between the pitches a candidate implies and those
heard in the audio is a natural next source. Code and trained scorers will be
released.
